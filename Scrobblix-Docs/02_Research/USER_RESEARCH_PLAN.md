# User Research Plan

> **Project:** Scrobblix  
> **Owner:** Rahul Muraleedharan (`frpboy`)  
> **Status:** Documentation baseline  
> **Last updated:** 2026-08-01


## Objectives

- Identify the most common missed-scrobble scenarios.
- Validate onboarding and permission explanations.
- Test whether users understand local versus Last.fm status.
- Determine which diagnostics are actionable without technical knowledge.
- Prioritize first-party browser adapters.

## Participant mix

Recruit 12–20 users across casual Last.fm users, browser-first listeners, multi-player power users, privacy-conscious users, and technical contributors. Include current Windows 11 versions and major Chromium/Firefox browsers.

## Sessions

### Discovery interview

Ask participants to reconstruct a recent missing-scrobble event, existing tools, trust level, players/sites, workarounds, and privacy concerns.

### Prototype usability

Tasks:

1. Connect Last.fm.
2. Determine whether a current track will scrobble.
3. Explain why a short track did not scrobble.
4. Recover from expired authentication.
5. Find a track from yesterday and export it.
6. Enable a browser site without granting unrelated access.

### Diary study

For one week, participants mark moments when Scrobblix behaved unexpectedly. The app can create a local diagnostic bookmark without uploading data.

## Evidence capture

- Task completion and time.
- Misinterpretations.
- Permission hesitation.
- Diagnostic reason selected.
- Source/website compatibility.
- Resource-use complaints.

## Ethics and privacy

Use test accounts where possible. Do not request complete listening exports. Redact usernames, track history, and filesystem paths in research artifacts unless participants explicitly consent.
